# Load report — 2026-08-12, Phase 4 baseline

The first numbers. Everything ran on one machine (Apple Silicon laptop, the full
compose stack plus the observability overlay, k6 in a container on the same Docker
network), which bounds what these figures can claim: they are the platform's *shape*
under load — where it bends, what breaks first, how it recovers — not a capacity
statement about production hardware. The simulated acquirers answer in 32–68 ms by
configuration, which is realistic for a domestic acquirer and generous for a slow one.

Scenarios: `deploy/k6/` · runner: `./scripts/load.sh` · k6 1.1.0 streaming into
Prometheus, so every graph referenced here is reproducible on the provisioned
dashboards from a cold start.

## Headline results

| Scenario | Volume | Failed requests | Creation p99 (client) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Steady, 20 rps × 5 m | 6 001 | 0 | **10.8 ms** (SLO < 150 ms) | Pass |
| Spike, 10→50 rps | 5 400 | 0 | 46.1 ms at peak | Pass |
| Brownout under load, 15 rps × 5 m | 4 504 | 0 | p95 6.3 ms | Pass |

Service-side, the steady run's creation p99 was 9.0 ms — the client-side number
carries one Docker network hop. Full-lifecycle traces (create → authorize → capture →
ledger postings, sampled from Tempo across all four services) ran p50 251 ms /
p95 578 ms against the 2 s authorization SLO; most of that is the two acquirer calls
plus the outbox relay's sub-second hops, which is the asynchrony working as designed.

## Brownout under load: what the thresholds don't show

The scenario browns out `southcross` — carrier of ~75% of the corridor — for two of
the five minutes. Every HTTP request succeeded, but the *eventual* outcomes are the
honest measure, read from the payments funnel over the window:

| | |
|---|---|
| Payments created | ≈ 4 142 |
| Eventually authorized | ≈ 4 108 (**99.35%**) |
| Failed after exhausting bounded retries | ≈ 27 (0.65%) |
| Captures failed softly back to `AUTHORIZED` | ≈ 41 |

Mid-brownout (T+130 s), the router had already cut the victim to a ~23% attempt share
and falling, and the **retry budget was 96% utilised** — at demo scale the brownout
never touches the budget, but at 15 rps the cascading failovers consume nearly all of
it, and the 27 failures are the payments the budget declined to fight for. That is
the design choosing platform stability over individual payments, and 99.35% is within
the SLO's two points of the healthy acquirers' rate — but it is not the demo's 100%,
and the difference is worth knowing about before an incident makes it known.

The ~41 failed captures are Phase 3's "a brownout strands captures" finding at load:
captures are pinned to the authorizing acquirer, failover cannot help them, and they
fail softly back to `AUTHORIZED` for retry once the acquirer heals.

## The bottleneck: found, measured, moved

Probing above the scenarios found the ceiling exactly where the architecture predicts
it: **the router's listener threads are the authorization throughput limit**, because
each one holds an acquirer call (~45 ms healthy) while payment-api's front door — one
fast database transaction per request — accepts almost arbitrarily more.

**Before** — at 100 rps with listener concurrency 3: HTTP stayed perfect (p99 4.3 ms,
zero failures) while the router consumed at **~49 authorizations/s** and the surplus
became consumer lag, growing linearly past 2 900 records by run end and taking
another ~90 s to drain afterwards. Every lagged record is a customer watching a
spinner behind a 202.

**The change** — listener concurrency 3 → 6 on the router, one thread per partition:
the headroom ADR-0005 provisioned when it chose six partitions, now actually used.

**After** — the same 100 rps probe: lag oscillated between 0 and ~480 within fetch
batches and was **zero before the run finished**; creation p99 5.4 ms. The consumer
now keeps pace with the front door at five times the steady scenario's rate.

Past this, more threads stop helping — six threads own six partitions — so the next
ceiling is broken by more router instances (the relay and attempt-claiming were built
multi-instance from Phase 1) or more partitions, both deliberate changes rather than
tuning. payment-api and ledger consumers never lagged at any tested rate; their
handlers are millisecond database operations with no blocking calls.

## Caveats, so this report can be trusted

- One laptop ran everything: the load generator, the platform, and the observability
  stack share cores, so absolute latencies flatter no one and the *ratios* are the
  portable result.
- The acquirer simulator is well-behaved even when "unhealthy" — real acquirers
  produce long tails and connection weirdness that no toxic here reproduced.
- 202-rate is a threshold; eventual-outcome acceptance is the number that matters,
  and this report states both because they diverged (100% vs 99.35%) exactly when it
  got interesting.
- The retry-budget saturation at 15 rps suggests the 10% ratio deserves a revisit
  with production-shaped traffic before anyone trusts it at scale — filed in the
  backlog rather than tuned on the spot.
